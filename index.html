Tier 1 (airline announcement, April 23). 120 daily services already disappearing from schedules from 20 April 2026 . Type 4 Distribution FM emerging.

Qatalum aluminium output will remain at around 60% capacity after QatarEnergy confirmed it would continue supplying gas to the smelter at reduced levels . Tier 1 (QatarEnergy/Norsk Hydro statement). Damage-control outcome; prevents full shutdown restart risk (6–12 months). Stable near baseline.

Strait traffic · 3.3% of normal · single ceasefire transit

Traffic is running at a pathetic 3.3% of normal . Qatar LNG cargoes resumed transiting Hormuz for the first time since the February closure on May 9 under ceasefire framework. Two additional Qatar-linked LNG vessels remain idled near Qatari waters after being turned back by the IRGC on April 6. No updated transit clearance has been confirmed for either vessel. Tier 1 (shipping data). Marginal improvement, not normalization.

Single-transit ceasefire gesture insufficient to restore commercial confidence. Chokepoint remains effectively closed.
Escalate

Crude/product inventories · 45-day coverage · critical threshold

Global crude inventories are now approaching their lowest level in nearly eight years, and stocks of refined products such as gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel are projected to cover only about 45 days of demand . Tier 1 (Goldman Sachs estimate, cited in May 12 source). Physical supply constraint visible. 45-day buffer is critically low.

Global energy system moving toward stock exhaustion risk if Hormuz closure extends beyond June. Demand destruction from aviation cuts + maritime fuel rationing now visible mitigation pathway. June–July is critical inflection point.
