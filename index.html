Category status board

Six categories · 72h pulse
Geopolitical

Trump-Pezeshkian MOU · Strait Reopening Formal

The memorandum was signed 18 June by President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian. The deal includes a 60-day commitment to further talks, a $300 billion plan for Iran's reconstruction, and the removal of "all types" of U.S. sanctions. It temporarily reopens the Strait, eases sanctions via waivers, and sets off a new round of high-stakes talks.

Signal type: Hard (sovereign-level commitment). Tier 1 source: Trump administration / Iranian state. Implication: Institutional reopening formalised; operational transits now legally permitted under PGSA framework.
Maritime / Procedure

PGSA 48-Hour Advance Notice Rule · 20 June

Iran ordered all vessels to submit transit requests at least 48 hours in advance through the PGSA; only compliant vessels will be cleared without delay. Ships must follow designated routes and coordinate timing, citing safety concerns tied to mine-affected areas. The PGSA requires passage permits and hull war voyage insurance; applications must be submitted 48 hours before departure, with captains receiving route coordinates 24 hours prior.

Signal type: Medium (regulatory procedure, not FM declaration). Tier 1 source: PGSA official statement via Marine Insight (20 June). Implication: Administrative control formalised; introduces operational friction (48h window) and sanctions compliance trap post-60-day fee window.
Shipping / Restart

Saudi VLCC Transits Resume · 19 June

Three Saudi VLCCs successfully transited the Strait on 19 June and reactivated AIS transponders in the Gulf of Oman. These vessels had suppressed AIS for over two months; the simultaneous reactivation was a coordinated decision. On a single Saturday following the US-Iran announcement, more than 20 vessels transited the strait—the highest single-day count since 1 March.

Signal type: Hard (physical asset movement + AIS confirmation). Tier 1 source: Kpler vessel-tracking data cited in Discovery Alert (19 June). Implication: Commercial shipping resuming under PGSA permit regime; asymmetry (Saudi passage vs. Iran-flagged free transit) narrowing; tanker fleet repositioning accelerating.
LNG Production

QatarEnergy Ras Laffan Restart Trajectory

QatarEnergy is ready to resume LNG production at Ras Laffan and could restore output from unaffected facilities to current capacity within a month. Two of Qatar's 14 LNG trains and one of its two GTL facilities were damaged during strikes, resulting in loss of around 17% of the country's LNG export capacity. QatarEnergy has told buyers it expects to raise output to about 50% of capacity a month after safe passage is restored, and to roughly 80% within two months.

Signal type: Hard (company forward guidance to buyers). Tier 1 source: Reuters / Bloomberg (16–17 June, sourced to people familiar). Implication: Unaffected trains (12 of 14) can ramp quickly; damaged Trains 4/6 require 3–5 yr rebuild; restart conditional on continuous Hormuz access.
Crude Oil / Allocation

KPC FM Lift Announced · 19 June

Kuwait Petroleum Company's managing director stated that Kuwait could recover 70% of its oil production within 6–8 weeks after the Strait reopens. The FM#2 declaration (issued early June, "even when Strait reopens") is now being lifted; CEO confirmed immediate production restoration to normal levels within weeks of international shipping resumption.

Signal type: Hard (first-party operator statement). Tier 1 source: KPC executive statement via Gulf Times (early June); FM lift confirmed 19 June. Implication: Allocation pressure easing in Gulf crude; Saudi/UAE/Iraq parallel ramp signals coordinated regional restart intent.
Energy Markets

Oil Price & Spreads Post-MOU

Brent closed at $83 the day after the deal and fell below $78 by 18 June—the war premium gone, and most of the institutional premium gone too. The U.S. stock market recently notched a fresh record high and oil prices have fallen on news of the Iran peace deal, although they remain significantly higher than prewar levels. EU naphtha spot prices (S&P Global Platts) fell ~10% from 17–22 June to ~$685/mt; ethylene in NE Asia down ~5% to $1,280/mt from 19 June highs.

Signal type: Medium (market price data). Tier 2 source: S&P Global Platts, Lloyd's List (22 June). Implication: Price shock unwinding post-MOU; forward curves now price 50% LNG ramp by 20 July, 80% by 20 August; structural premium remains for PGSA toll/fee risk post-August 17.
