Bunker Fuel Shortage

Type 4 Distribution Crisis Emerging

Bunker fuel prices surged to $800/tonne in early May 2026, a +60% increase from pre-war levels of ~$500/tonne, as Iraq and Kuwait crude supply was choked off by the Strait of Hormuz closure. Singapore, the world's largest refueling hub for maritime shipping, is experiencing reserve depletion. The shortage has not yet triggered operator-formalized FM declarations, but Tier 2 shipping industry sources (WFSB, Washington Post, Euronews) confirm physical tightness and rising alarm among container operators (MSC, Maersk, CMA CGM). This is the first explicit tertiary-industry (Type 4 Distribution) FM signal beyond production and allocation tiers.

Tier 2 · Emerging · May 12 signal · Physical scarcity · 80% of global trade exposed
QatarEnergy LNG Extension

FM Prolonged to Early July

QatarEnergy extended its force majeure on LNG supply through early July, canceling two additional cargoes to Italian buyer Edison on top of 10 already suspended since the war onset (28 February). Ras Laffan Trains 4 and 6 remain offline with extensive structural damage requiring up to 5 years to repair. The extension is now binding through Q2 2026 under customer notification. No restart timeline has been announced on primary LNG trains. This is the second major extension signal, reinforcing multi-quarter supply horizon embedded in bilateral pricing.

Tier 1 · Hard FM · QatarEnergy official · LNG production halted · 5-year rebuild horizon
Strait Closure Milestone

69 Days Functionally Closed

The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed to commercial shipping for 69+ days since the Hormuz crisis onset on 28 February 2026, following a failed brief ceasefire reopening on 21–22 April that collapsed within 24 hours. In May 2026, Iran redefined the Strait as a "vast operational area" under IRGC control, extending jurisdiction from the narrow maritime corridor to a broader strategic zone. US Project Freedom (announced 4 May) paused on 6 May "for progress toward a deal." Approximately 5% of pre-war vessel traffic is now transiting; ceasefire remains on "massive life support" per Trump (11 May). Shipping insurance and routing costs remain elevated; approximately 20% of global oil and 20% of global LNG normally transit this chokepoint.

Tier 1 · Geopolitical closure · IRGC redefinition · 69+ days · Regulatory uncertainty · ~5% normal traffic
Lotte Chemical Restart Delay

May 18 → May 29 Shift

Lotte Chemical's Yeosu plant restart has been officially delayed from 18 May to 29 May (originally moved up by 3 weeks from its scheduled June turnaround). The delay reflects continued naphtha feedstock tightness in Asia. Maybank analyst warnings (6 April) flagged June production halt risk if Middle Eastern naphtha supply does not resume; inventories at major crackers are typically 2–3 weeks of feedstock only. With the Strait closure now 69 days old, feedstock-dependent operators are beginning cascading stops rather than temporary shutdowns. This restart is now the operational test for Wave 3 unwind; confirmation or further delay will signal severity of downstream cracker stress.

Tier 2 · Restart signal · May 29 deadline · Naphtha inventory dependent · Asia cracker stress indicator
Naphtha Price Momentum

$897/T on 15 May; YoY +62.54%

Naphtha spot prices rose to $897/tonne on 15 May 2026 (+2.46% day-on-day), maintaining elevated levels driven by Hormuz closure and regional refinery shutdowns. Month-over-month prices have fallen 2% from their early-May spike, but remain +62.54% above May 2025 levels. European naphtha prices showed +19% Q4–Q1 2026 upward movement; Asia remained stable at 1.05 USD/KG during the same period, suggesting substitution flows toward Asian feedstock sourcing. Q1 2026 Saudi Arabia naphtha pricing rose +21.2% QoQ as exporters prioritized Asian nominations and spot availability contracted. The persistence of elevated pricing above $850/T locks multi-quarter cost assumptions into cracker economics, extending the restart timeline beyond simple reopening of the Strait.

Tier 2 · Commodity price · $897/T · YoY +62.54% · Cost inflation locked in · Asian pivot evident
Restart-Type FM Baseline

4 Operators · Multi-Quarter Language

Four operators have now declared or are operating under restart-type FM language (obligations binding "even when Strait reopens" or "cannot estimate return"): (1) QatarEnergy 5-year LNG FM, (2) KPC FM#2 sovereign crude FM, (3) SABIC Tadawul filing "cannot provide estimate for return to production", (4) EGA Al Taweelah 12-month aluminium smelter rebuild. These declarations anchor supply uncertainty to physical/contractual timelines independent of ceasefire. No new restart-type FM was declared in the 13–16 May window. This count is the leading indicator for transition from L4 Systemic to L5 Regime; any new operator-level restart-type FM extension (e.g., KNPC, BAPCO, Qatalum formal "multi-quarter" language) would signal regime shift.
