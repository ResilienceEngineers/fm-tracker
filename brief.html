The global maritime industry is facing a looming shortage of the fuel that powers ships as the Iran war chokes off a key shipping lane in the Strait of Hormuz. Day 78 closes with no new Hard operator FM declarations in the 72-hour window (13–16 May), and one explicit tertiary-industry signal: bunker fuel shortage has surfaced as a Type 4 Distribution-tier FM driver. Before the war, bunker fuel in Singapore cost about $500 per metric ton. That went up to more than $800 as of early May. In May 2026, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy announced that Iran had redefined the Strait of Hormuz into what officials described as a "vast operational area". State-backed QatarEnergy has informed Italian energy company Edison of the cancellation of two additional liquefied natural gas (LNG) cargoes and the extension of force majeure to early July. Lotte Chemical's restart has shifted from May 18 to May 29 due to continued naphtha feedstock tightness; Maybank analysts warn of June production halt risk if inventory depletes. The Strait has been functionally closed for 69+ days (since 28 February 2026 onset). No primary operator restart was confirmed in the trailing 72 hours. Restart-type FM count (multi-quarter "cannot estimate" and "even when reopens" language) remains static at 4. Trend: Same. Wave Intensity: L4 Systemic.

Substitution / Alternative Sourcing

Asia pivot ongoing · EU import constraints
  • Asian naphtha pricing stable; European +19% Q1 2026: Tier 2 pricing data (IMARC, ChemAnalyst) shows Asian crackers have absorbed feedstock scarcity without price escalation relative to Europe. This indicates substitution flows are rerouting Gulf naphtha toward Asia rather than Europe, compressing spreads in Northeast Asia (1.05 USD/KG stable) while European import parity widened +19%.
  • EU storage stress and Russian LNG substitution: Tier 1 data (31% of EU LNG from Yamal Jan–Apr 2026, per Kpler analysis cited in Wikipedia) confirms Europe is substituting Russian LNG for lost Qatar/Gulf supplies. This creates dependency inversion: EU energy security is now hostage to Russian/Arctic infrastructure.
  • North America dual-fuel advantage emerging: EIA May 2026 STEO reports 0.9 Bcf/d new LNG capacity added April (Golden Pass Train 1, 0.7 Bcf/d). US LNG is gaining market share as buyers accept higher cost to avoid Strait risk. Asian buyers are also sourcing US naphtha and ethane crackers as substitutes for Hormuz feedstock.
Why this matters: Substitution is occurring, but it is asymmetric: Asia is absorbing Hormuz closures through price discipline and redirected flows; Europe is becoming dependent on Russian supply; North America is capturing incremental LNG demand. This creates a structural bifurcation in supply chains: Middle East-dependent industries (aluminium, fertilizer, helium) cannot substitute; petrochemical producers (naphtha crackers) can reroute but at cost and logistical friction.
Implication: Substitution is not a long-term solution. Asia's naphtha price stability is masking inventory draws (confirmed: 2–3 weeks feedstock left at May 16). Once inventory is depleted, prices will spike into European levels (+$150–200/tonne). EU dependence on Russian LNG (98% of Yamal exports Jan–Apr 2026) creates geopolitical risk for H2 2026 if EU–Russia relations deteriorate. Industries exposed to the Strait (aluminium, crude, helium) have NO substitutes; expect these to remain in FM through 2027.

Methodology

Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened.
