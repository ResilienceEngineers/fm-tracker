Substitution / alternative sourcing

US LNG, Aramco Red Sea bypass, gov't stockpiles active
  • Aramco East-West Pipeline (Petroline) ramp-up (Tier 1, ongoing): Aramco has ramped up the pipeline's capacity to 7 million barrels per day . This bypasses Hormuz for Saudi crude to Red Sea. Combined with Egypt's Sumed pipeline surge of 150% providing alternative route for oil transport from Red Sea , alternative routing is offsetting ~7 Mbbl/d of the 20 Mbbl/d pre-war Hormuz flow. Remaining ~13 Mbbl/d gap persists; no closure-route alternative.
  • US LNG export ramp (Tier 1, May STEO): US LNG export capacity grew ~0.9 Bcf/d in April (Golden Pass Train 1 first shipment, Corpus Christi Stage 3); Corpus Christi Train 6 scheduled summer 2026, adding 0.2 Bcf/d . Incremental but not gap-filling for 30+ Bcf/d Qatar/Australia/Malaysia exports.
  • IEA / US Strategic Reserve release (Tier 1, ongoing): 400M barrels released by 32 nations. Steepest inventory draws projected in May and June, keeping Brent elevated at ~$106/b . Temporary buffer, not structural solution.
Why this matters — Substitution is fully mobilized but can only offset ~50% of Hormuz gap (7 Mbbl/d pipeline bypass + 0.4 Mbbl/d LNG incremental + 0.8 Mbbl/d strategic reserves release ≈ 8 Mbbl/d vs 13 Mbbl/d gap). This means Strait reopening is the binding constraint; no amount of substitution closes the gap for extended closure.
Implication — If Strait reopens in late May per EIA baseline, alternative sourcing gradually winds down. If Strait closure extends to June/July, substitution capacity is exhausted, and primary-demand destruction becomes the market-clearing mechanism (rationing, industrial curtailment, recession risk).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
