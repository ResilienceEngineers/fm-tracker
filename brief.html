Kinetic / facility damage

East-West pipeline repair in progress
  • 10–11 Sept drone strikes hit East-West pipeline; Saudi Arabia shut it down as precautionary measure, removing main alternative export route while Strait closed . Pump Station 11 damaged; satellite imagery confirmed fires at multiple sites (Copernicus Sentinel-3, NASA FIRMS).
  • Repairs including major pumping facility damage could take 3–5 weeks per regional officials briefed on the matter . Implies restart window of late September to early October if timeline holds.
  • Pipeline moved 2.6–4 Mbpd weekly since late Aug . Full loss of capacity during repair window exposes Saudi inventory to rapid depletion; per analyst consensus, 5–7 day cushion at current export rate.
Why this matters
The East-West closure introduces a second independent critical-infrastructure failure vector unrelated to Hormuz maritime blockade. This dual-system failure (maritime + terrestrial) is the key L4→L5 boundary test. A third infrastructure hit (desalination, port terminal, crude pipeline alternate) within 72h would confirm multi-vector attack pattern and trigger L5.
Implication
Saudi crude export disruption now depends on two repair timelines: (1) East-West (3–5 weeks) and (2) Strait reopening (negotiation-dependent, no announced breakthrough). If East-West delays past Oct 10 and Strait remains blocked, Saudi exports could drop from current 6 Mbpd to 3–4 Mbpd by mid-October, forcing premium crude rationing and Brent spike above $110. Watch for announcement of partial pipeline restart by 24 September.

Substitution / alternative sourcing

LNG: US diversion sustains; naphtha alternatives limited
  • Edison sourced seven US LNG cargoes after ten shipments cancelled April–June; CEO Monti confirmed capacity to replace all affected cargoes from US and spot market . Recent US LNG onstream (Plaquemines, Golden Pass partial capacity) providing marginal supply; spot LNG prices ($15.50 per MMBtu JKM, vs. pre-war $10.70) reflect marginal-cost substitution.
  • Naphtha substitution: EU crackers pivoting to alternatives (propane, crude-run flexibility) but at 15–20% yield penalty. INEOS Styrolution and Trinseo reported margin compression 16–19 Sept per sell-side analyst notes (Citi, Credit Suisse, Bank of America). No formal FM declarations, but margin erosion signals latent stress.
  • Methanol: Ar-Razi (Qatar, offline since March) remains unrepaired. Methanex Damietta (Egypt, offline since March) sustained production halt. Asian crackers absorbing loss via Saudi/Chinese methanol (Saudi Aramco Yanbu, Sipchem, and PetroChina Panjin). No new supplier FM filed 16–19 Sept.
Why this matters
Substitution is elastic but not infinite. US LNG diversion works until Plaquemines and Golden Pass ramp to capacity (expected Q4 2026); naphtha alternatives (propane, crude) have 10–20% yield penalties that erode operator margins; methanol and methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) have no practical substitutes. Exhaustion of alternatives signals proximity to Type 3 / Type 4 cascade.
Implication
Edison's ability to replace 17 of 24 LNG cargoes reflects market depth at this moment. If Hormuz closure extends past December or East-West repair fails, spot LNG and naphtha supplies will tighten. Watch Platts JKM price for breach above $18 per MMBtu (marginal-cost signal of supply exhaustion). Watch Trinseo / INEOS margin reports for 16–23 September for statements on forward pricing power.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
