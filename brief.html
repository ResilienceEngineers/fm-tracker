Saudi Arabia diverted oil to the Red Sea port of Yanbu via the East–West Crude Oil Pipeline, and the UAE diverted oil via the Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline to the port of Fujairah.
  • Aluminium Bahrain (Alba), which produces 1.6 million tonnes annually, is considering using Sohar, and is exploring Saudi Arabia's Red Sea port of Jeddah as another route. ALBA, EGA, QatarEnergy using same bypasses. Capacity constraints: East-West pipeline + Sohar + Yanbu + Jeddah + Fujairah = ~3–4 Mbbl/d combined, vs. normal Hormuz 20 Mbbl/d.
  • Saudi Arabia crude oil production decreased to 7.763 million barrels per day in March from 10.111 million barrels per day in February 2026. Saudi output down sharply due to export constraints (bypass routes at capacity, tankers can't leave Gulf easily). No new substitution agreements (alternative suppliers) announced in 72h.
Why this matters Bypass routes are operational but bottlenecked. Saudi Arabian output is down 23% YoY because producers can't export fast enough through Red Sea routes, not because production capacity has been destroyed. This is a shipping/logistics constraint, not a production constraint — it's reversible if Strait reopens. However, it signals that even dual-routing strategies (Hormuz + Red Sea + Sohar) are insufficient to normalize supply. For every barrel that reroutes, one stays in Gulf storage or remains unproduced. Long-term substitution (alternative suppliers, new LNG plants, new crackers) takes 2–3 years and requires certainty on Strait status. No operator will greenlight capex while Hormuz remains disputed.
Implication Alternative sourcing (US shale, North Sea, Australian LNG, Canadian oil) has already begun but at a slow pace — it is too expensive to redirect infrastructure mid-cycle. Watch for: (1) new capacity ramp announcements from US Gulf Coast, Canada, Australia (signaling market bet on "Strait will stay closed long-term"); (2) long-term offtake agreements between Gulf NOCs and Red Sea / Indian Ocean partners (Saudi-to-Egypt pipeline expansion, Qatar-to-India LNG); (3) carrier insurance costs and route-economics decisions (rerouting adds 14–21 days and 15–25% extra fuel cost). If no new agreements or capex authorizations by June 15, substitute-sourcing is NOT becoming structural; Strait reopening is still the base-case assumption.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.
