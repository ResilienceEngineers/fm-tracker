Substitution / alternative sourcing

  • China battery export controls expiry (November 2026 risk event): That suspension expires in November 2026, creating a direct overlap with the November 10 deadline; the November 2026 expiry of China's suspended export controls on battery supply chain materials, including cathode precursors, graphite, and battery manufacturing equipment, is the risk event that sits above all current price action in the battery metals complex; the November deadline is the date that forces the entire downstream battery supply chain outside China to confront the gap between where it needs to be and where it actually is in terms of midstream processing independence (Silmaril Media, 16 Oct, Tier 3 forward analysis). China cathode precursor export ban reinstatement would bifurcate battery supply chain (onshore China vs. offshore). Lithium carbonate: CNY 140,000/t (6 Nov, stable); cobalt: 25,000 yuan/mt rebound this week post-weakness (SMM 6 Nov, Tier 2).
  • DRC cobalt supply enforcement crackdown (3 Nov): Congo seizes copper, cobalt in illegal mining crackdown; authorities confiscate hundreds of vehicles and thousands of tons of minerals as Congo steps up enforcement at mine sites (Mining.com, 3 Nov, Tier 2 news). Note: regulatory crackdown (not production FM), but supply-side effect similar. Alternative sourcing: Bougouni (Mali) lithium 5th cargo reached Hainan 8 Nov (17,400 tonnes, Tier 2 SMM), reflecting substitution away from Australia/Argentina dominance; however subject to China downstream processing control.
  • US West Coast ILWU Local 26 port labour strike threat (extended to 20 Nov): ILWU Local 26 reached an agreement to extend their contract through Nov. 20 and will hold additional talks during that period; the Pacific Maritime Association (PMA) and International Longshore & Warehouse Union (ILWU) Local 26 reached an agreement to extend their contract through Nov. 20 and will hold additional talks during that period (Feedstuffs, 9 Nov; Sobel Network, 9 Nov, Tier 1 labour agreement). Local 26 represents about 250 watchmen who provide security at six container terminals at the Los Angeles and Long Beach ports; although they make up just 2% of the overall ILWU workforce at the ports, these terminals handle more than half of the total cargo, including roughly 85% at Long Beach and 33% at Los Angeles (PMA statement, Tier 1). Escalation risk: if strike triggered 20 Nov, vessel/yard/gate ops halt at 6–8 terminals; other ILWU members historically honour picket lines (Tier 1 precedent).
Why this matters China battery export controls November expiry is a standalone policy FM event (hormuz_linked=False) that will constrain EV/energy-storage supply chains globally; reinstatement would split cathode precursor markets into two tiers (China-domestic favored, offshore penalized). DRC cobalt crackdown adds regulatory enforcement layer to commodity supply, not kinetic disruption. US ILWU Local 26 strike threshold (20 Nov) is 10 days away; LA–LB handles ~15% of US containerized trade; strike would ripple to rail, truck, inland distribution networks within 48h of trigger.
Implication China export-control reinstatement risk (11–20 Nov window) will drive battery-metal hedging and precursor spot-market hoarding Week 1. DRC crackdown amplifies cobalt supply tightness already present from mine-maintenance FMs. ILWU Local 26 strike probability rises if negotiations fail by 20 Nov; if triggered, US container shipping backlog will exceed prior 2024 ILA east-coast strike impact (handled only 2% of US volume; 2024 strike affected 43–49% of US container volume). LA–LB supply-chain impact: export containerization for East Asia/EU shipments will face 3–5 day queuing if Local 26 strikes; inland rail/truck capacity will saturate by day 3. Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
