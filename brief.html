Cascade through downstream chains

DRC cobalt supply shock; chemical producer cost pressure (US Gulf)

Supply side, manufacturers faced insufficient raw materials and low inventory, with strong sentiment to hold back from selling. Battery, EV, semiconductor, and aerospace supply chains exposed; cobalt intermediate products (Co3O4) jumped to $15.5–16.5/lb.
  • US Gulf gas cost pressure (SNG pipeline FM): The pipeline explosion and force majeure declaration could tighten natural gas transportation availability in Alabama and surrounding markets, potentially increasing regional gas prices if the outage persists.   • QatarEnergy LNG supply tightness extends into Q1 2027: US and African supply covered up to two-thirds of the shortfall, but QatarEnergy's force majeure runs to at least the end of December 2026, so 2027 tightness is the base case until Hormuz shipping and European storage normalise. Asian and European LNG buyers face continued scarcity pricing through 2027 if Ras Laffan rebuild timeline extends.
Why this matters: DRC cobalt ban is a non-Hormuz global FM with critical-minerals cascade implications; independent of Hormuz crisis but compounds existing supply-chain tightness from Hormuz-linked LNG/fertilizer/specialty-chemical outages. US Gulf gas cost pressure is regional (Alabama/Southeast) but affects ammonia and methanol producers whose feedstock inputs are already stressed by Middle East naphtha/LNG shortages. Combined cascade: energy cost + raw-material cost + substitution constraints = profitability pressure on gas-intensive chemistry (ammonia, methanol, hydrogen).
Implication: Watch for near-term buyer cancellations or force majeure from US ammonia/methanol producers in December if SNG outage persists beyond 2 weeks. Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
