Asymmetric posture; claim lacks operational credibility test.

JMIC reported no AIS-visible transits by cargo-carrying vessels above 1,000 deadweight tonnes between November 19 and 21. JMIC's internal tracking counted approximately 218 commercial vessels stationary at major anchorages east of the Strait of Hormuz. Shipping throughput 65% below pre-war baseline (13 Nov UKMTO report).

Cascade through Downstream Chains

Tier 1 · Wave 3 Feedstock
  • Egypt fertilizer gas curtailment increased 20%→33% (8 November effective). Egypt increased curtailments on natural gas supplied to domestic fertilizer producers on November 8, raising cuts from 20% to 33% as costly liquefied natural gas imports strain the country's domestic gas balance. Abu Qir, KIMA, MOPCO affected. Downstream: Nile Delta wheat zone, East Africa grain exports dependent on Egyptian DAP/urea feedstock.
  • Pakistan urea shortfall 200,000 tonnes (disclosed 17 November parliament). Pakistan's government acknowledged a fertiliser shortfall of more than 200,000 tonnes against national demand, a gap officials attributed to gas supply disruptions tied to the Middle East conflict that forced two domestic urea plants to shut down temporarily. Pakistan's domestic urea industry runs almost entirely on natural gas as feedstock. Khan's disclosure places fertiliser availability back at the center of a debate that recurs most years around Rabi sowing, when demand for urea is at its highest. Rabi wheat sowing (Nov–Dec) compressed timeline.
  • QAFCO, Ar-Razi, Qatar Industries offline; Indian LNG sourcing cut. Qatar's state-owned fertilizer producer QAFCO shut down its urea plant with an annual capacity of 5.6 million tons over energy supply disruptions. The Strait of Hormuz led to a 33% contraction in the global fertilizer supply chain, while the region's annual urea exports of 22 million tons halted. Some 46% of global urea supply is sourced directly from the Gulf region.
Why this matters Fertilizer cascade is now a Wave 3 (physical absence of feedstock) signature: not a temporary price shock (Wave 1) or logistics re-routing (Wave 2), but a multi-quarter production shutdown in LNG-dependent urea/ammonia plants. Egypt's 33% gas cut + Pakistan's 200k-t shortfall + Qatar/Iran offline = ~40% global urea supply disruption. Rabi 2026–27 (North India wheat sowing) and 2026–27 African maize cycles face critical nitrogen availability loss. An American Farm Bureau survey of US farmers earlier this year found that around 70% of respondents said they were unable to afford all the fertilizer needed for their crops in 2026. Food price expectations for 2027 rising; emerging markets face affordability crisis. Russian phosphate export quota cut (41 million t → 18.8 million t, effective Dec 1 per source) compounded shortage.
Implication Global agricultural input cost index up 25–35% year-on-year into 2027. Expect India Ministry of Agriculture to announce domestic urea production support policy by 27 Nov (subsidies or government-sourced imports). African countries (Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria) to submit emergency humanitarian fertilizer requests to UN by early December. Food inflation expectations revised upward in markets pricing 2027 grain availability; wheat futures (Chicago CBOT) hold elevated premium on reduced northern-hemisphere supply. Wave 3 duration = 2–3 years per baseline (Ras Laffan rebuild timeline); fertilizer affordability crisis persists Q1–Q4 2027 minimum.
Sources · Egypt State Gas Authority / Abu Qir disclosures (Nov 8–16); Pakistan Parliamentary Question Hour (17 Nov); Reuters (Global Agriculture); World Bank fertilizer analysis; Rabobank (24 Nov) · Tier 1

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
