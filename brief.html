Substitution / Alternative Sourcing

LNG (US expansion), Petrochemicals (West Coast routing), Energy (spot)
  • QatarEnergy turns to US LNG (29 Oct): QatarEnergy announced 20-year deal with Woodside for 3 Mt/yr from Louisiana (first deliveries 2030), making QatarEnergy largest buyer of US LNG; adds to Golden Pass stake in Texas and Cheniere deal signed last week . Tier 1 signal of long-term redirection away from Ras Laffan. Operator is hedging by becoming a buyer of US capacity, implying no confidence in Ras Laffan recovery timeline.
  • Saudi Aramco western-port rerouting (through 29 Oct): Crude exports 7.9 mbpd (first 29 Oct) comprised 3.32 mbpd west coast, 4.55 mbpd east; 7.9 vs. 5.8 Sept baseline . Western ports (Yanbu, Sidi Kerir via Egypt) now carry ~42% of Aramco's total, confirming substitution of Red Sea routes for Hormuz.
  • Petronet propane tender (28 Oct): Petronet LNG opened propane supply for Dahej complex; describes propane as readily available, Gulf market disrupted . Feedstock substitution underway for downstream crackers.
Why this matters: Substitution signals indicate market is adapting to regime persistence. Substitution choices (US LNG, western ports, propane vs. LNG) are economically rational *and* implicitly accept that Hormuz blockade is durable. QatarEnergy's decision to become a buyer of US LNG is a 20-year commitment to US-based supply, signalling operator has de facto written off Ras Laffan as primary export source for next decade. Aramco's western-port rerouting (42% of crude) is working (7.9 mbpd vs. 5.8 Sept) but is expensive (insurance + shipping + overland): the premium is baked into high spot prices. Petronet's propane sourcing is 3-month–level substitution, not long-term; if Dahej cracker needs LNG for extended period, propane is not a scalable feedstock (limited surplus capacity globally).
Implication: Substitution success on Aramco export side (western ports working) is masking underlying fragility: the workaround is costly and vulnerable to IRGC expansion (internet cable threat, Oman coastal enforcement). QatarEnergy's US LNG deal is net-negative for Qatar's economic position (20-year revenue transfer to US operators, admission of Ras Laffan write-down) but is operationally sound. Petronet propane is a bridge, not a solution. For EU chemical industry, substitution options are exhausted: spot LNG is 2–3× expensive, pipeline gas from Russia/North Africa is blocked, storage is depleted. Winter 2026–27 will test whether European demand destruction + US LNG export expansion + Arab GCC/Egypt redirects can meet EU needs. If not, cascading industrial shutdowns in Feb–Mar 2027 become probable, triggering L5 regime signal (sustained production FM from EU chemical sector).

Scenario B — Escalation to L5 Regime Transition (20% probability): IRGC internet-cable strike executed early November, degrading maritime comms and triggering US carrier strike response. Probability increased from 15% (Day 243) to 20% by IRGC enforcement-area expansion threat and Houthi attack frequency uptick (29–30 Oct).

Scenario C — De-escalation / Partial Reopening (15% probability): Diplomatic breakthrough by mid-November (Pakistan mediation, Qatar–Iran bilateral). Iran permits partial Hormuz reopening (~50% normal traffic) in exchange for US blockade lift. QatarEnergy announces interim 1.5 Mt/yr export restart (damaged-train-only capacity). Gas prices fall to 50–70 EUR/MWh; spot LNG premiums compress. Restart-type FM count drops to 3–4. This is the optimistic path: diplomatic resolution + partial supply recovery within 2026. Probability static at 15%; no new signals surfaced this run to increase likelihood (IRGC enforcement expansion actually decreased probability).

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
