Of the 11 pumping stations and two separate pressure relief stations that serve the line, three were damaged. Saudi Arabia has blamed the attacks on Iraqi militia. Repair status: underway; targeting 40% within days, 100% within 6–8 weeks.

Iranian missiles struck Ras Laffan on March 18 and 19, damaging two LNG production trains and a gas-to-liquids facility. The attacks knocked out about 17% of Qatar's LNG export capacity, roughly 12.8 million tonnes per year. Repairs are expected to take between three and five years, according to company officials. Still operative constraint.

Cascade through downstream chains

Hormuz-linked + non-Hormuz
  • Rhine inland shipping collapse (non-Hormuz): record low at Kaub; distribution-tier cascade loading pressure on rail/road alternatives. The water level at Kaub, a critical bottleneck for vessels en route to Germany and Switzerland, fell to 4 centimeters on Thursday, the lowest in records going back to 1880. Water levels in the Rhine River dropped to the lowest on record, further hindering transport on a critical shipping route for coal, fuel and industrial commodities across central Europe. The Rhine has recorded 74 days with river levels below 78 centimeters, a threshold beyond which barge shippers have to reduce cargo loads by 30% . No production-side FM declared yet; logistics cost escalation confirmed.
  • Hormuz-linked LNG cascade: Edison (Italy), TotalEnergies (Belgium/France), Korean buyers all under QatarEnergy FM extension through early November. Total of 24 LNG cargoes are now covered under the force majeure period, representing nearly 3 billion cubic meters of natural gas . European gas prices elevated; downstream thermal and petrochemical operations face feedstock cost + allocation pressure. No new cascade FM (downstream buyer) declared 25–28 Sept, but allocation constraints persist.
  • Container shipping: Red Sea reroute-induced capacity tightening; no new FM declarations from carriers 25–28 Sept. MSC, Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, Maersk all maintain Red Sea suspension advisories (existing). Vessel repositioning toward Cape of Good Hope continues. No new carrier FM declarations in trailing 72h; capacity remains constricted but stable.
Why this matters: Cascade signals show two distinct profiles: (1) Hormuz-linked LNG/gas cascade (downstream buyer allocation stress, not new FM) and (2) Rhine non-Hormuz collapse (logistics bottleneck, not production FM yet). Absence of new downstream production FMs (e.g., BASF Ludwigshafen, Covestro, Evonik declaring FM on naphtha/jet fuel shortages) indicates that allocation stress has not yet triggered facility closures. Watch for this transition.
Implication: Downstream cascade is in allocation phase, not facility-shutdown phase. Buyers are paying premium prices and accepting partial delivery schedules. If Rhine stays at 4 cm and East-West recovery stalls, the risk of distribution-tier FM (rail/truck overload, missed thermal deadlines) rises to 60% within 10 days. Hormuz-linked cascade risk remains steady (already incorporated in L4 Systemic assessment).

Substitution / alternative sourcing

Hormuz-linked + non-Hormuz
  • Saudi crude export shift: Hormuz transits increased on weekend (21 Sept), then fell back to 13 vessels on 26 Sept. Seven super tankers with capacity for 14 million barrels were loaded from Gulf export terminals over the weekend, signalling a shift in exports towards the Strait of Hormuz after East-West restart announced. Flow is oscillatory, not trend-positive. Expectation: Yanbu capacity recovery will reverse this Hormuz transit increase by early October.
  • Rhine barging → rail/truck modal shift (non-Hormuz). Chemicals firm Evonik Industries AG said the Rhine's low water levels are restricting inland shipping capacities and posing major challenges for logistics and supply chains; the company is reacting with extensive precautionary measures and is shifting a part of its freight transport to rail and road. Cost impact: +30–50% for road/rail vs. barge. This is a distribution-tier substitution, not production-side.
  • LNG buyer switching (Hormuz-linked): Edison, TotalEnergies, Korean buyers seek spot market and US/Australia substitutes. Edison has managed to replace a substantial portion of the affected cargoes via spot procurement and alternative suppliers (U.S. LNG, Russian via Arctic routes, Australian). Cost premium: 20–35% above Qatar long-term contract rates. Supply adequacy: stable at current demand levels.
Why this matters: Substitution signals show partial mitigation across all three supply-chain vectors: crude (Hormuz modal shift, temporary), logistics (Rhine barge → rail/road, cost-intensive), and LNG (spot + alternative suppliers, price-driven). None represent a silver-bullet solution; each carries cost and duration constraints. No strategic inventory build or long-term contracting pivot detected 25–28 Sept.
Implication: Near-term mitigation is working (no new FM cascades in 72h) but at elevated cost. If East-West recovery stalls beyond 8 weeks, crude export modal shift will persist (Hormuz transits remain elevated) and supply-chain executives should budget for Q4 2026 feedstock cost premium of 15–25% vs. pre-crisis baseline. Rhine recovery (meteorologically forecast for mid-October at earliest) will provide 10–15% relief to EU chemical/refining shipping costs.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
