Why this matters Wave 3 cascade tail is still propagating but at reduced velocity. The absence of new FM declarations and the stability of retail product prices suggest (a) supply networks have built buffer inventory or (b) buyers have absorbed cost and shifted to substitute suppliers (Australia naphtha for Asian crackers, US LNG for Europe via long-term contract renegotiations, Russian aluminium for non-sanctioned markets). If this holds, the systemic risk transitions from production-shock to price-level burden on downstream manufacturing margins. The critical boundary is the 26–31 July window: if HSC activity and corridor routing don't re-escalate, downstream cascades may stabilize at current buffered levels. If new kinetic events occur or formal Strait closure is declared, expect Wave 3 FM surge from (a) retail-tier manufacturers (pharma excipients, food packaging, auto suppliers) by 1–2 August.

(2) Kinetic pace re-escalates (HSC >200 craft/day or new strike confirmed) → 2–4 new production FMs within 48h, LNG transits drop to <0.1, European gas spike to €50+/MWh, Wave 3 FM surge expected 1–3 August.   • Naphtha substitution (Asia): Korean crackers (Yeochun, Lotte, LG) confirming naphtha purchases from Russia (Rosneft, SIBUR) at <15% discount vs Middle East naphtha due to EU/US sanctions discount. Delivery lag: 10–14 days vs normal 3–5 days Hormuz route. Suppliers note "force majeure on Russian exports unknown"—logistics chains are not yet stressed but risk is latent. No new crackers announced alternate sources 19–25 July.
  • Aluminium displacement (global): China's primary aluminium production ramping (CCME reports +3% YoY June production) to fill ALBA/EGA/Qatalum loss (~6% of global capacity). Russia and Australia also absorbing quota via premium pricing. LME price at $2,800/tonne stable; no supply scarcity yet. No new smelter startups or substitution routes confirmed 22–25 July.
Why this matters Substitute supply chains are holding because: (1) pre-crisis hedging by buyers (Australia LNG forward contracts, Russian naphtha swaps) was deployed immediately, (2) demand destruction in Europe (recession talk, manufacturing cutbacks) is absorbing 25–35% of the supply shortfall, and (3) prices have risen enough to incentivize ramp-up of previously mothballed capacity (Russia, China, Australia). This is the "softest" part of the cascade model—when hedges are exhausted (30–60 days from activation) or when new kinetic events disrupt substitute routes (e.g., IRGC threatens Red Sea or South China Sea shipping), new FMs will spike. Current 22–25 July period shows no strain in substitute logistics; this suggests operators have 30–60 days of breathing room before hitting hard constraints.
Implication If kinetic activity in other chokepoints (Red Sea, South China Sea, Strait of Malacca) escalates in the next 30 days, expect substitute supply routes to break down and new FMs from (a) downstream retailers unable to access Russian naphtha (EU sanctions compliance), (b) Australian LNG buyers hitting port congestion, and (c) power generators unable to stagger fuel switching fast enough. Lead indicator: watch for (1) new OFAC sanctions on Russian LNG / naphtha, (2) Australian port strike or congestion claim, or (3) Red Sea shipping incident claim. Any of these trigger immediate FM cascade. Current probability of substitute route disruption 26 July–31 August: 30%.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
