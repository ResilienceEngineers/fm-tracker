Substitution / alternative sourcing

Recycled aluminium, Asia naphtha, LNG arbitrage
  • Aluminium: EGA Jebel Ali + recycling assets + overseas inventory (Tier 1): EGA holds significant metal inventories in transit and storage within UAE and internationally; these reserves expected to provide short-term support to customers . EGA's Jebel Ali smelter, also in UAE but geographically separated from Al Taweelah, continued normal operations throughout conflict . EGA selling significant volumes of alumina feedstock, preserving cash flow while physical smelting capacity remains offline . Substitution pathway: Jebel Ali + recycled (EGA Eco Green acquisition in Italy) + Asia imports (Indonesia, Vietnam capacity expansions).
  • Naphtha: Asia regional supply + Chinese teapots (Tier 2): In Asia, many refiners reduced production to conserve feedstock; teapots leveraging deep discounts on Iranian and Russian crude . Regional petrochem crackers (Yeochun NCC, Chandra Asri) restarting with non-Hormuz feedstock (Russian pipeline, Iranian corridor via pipeline to Iraq/Syria, Asian refineries). Korean petrochemical restructuring (Lotte, LG, Hanwha) shifting toward lower capex / higher margin specialty polymers (away from commodity base chemicals).
  • LNG: Asia-only arbitrage + diversion from Europe (Tier 1): Asia absorbed 86% of LNG transiting via Hormuz and, in wake of attacks, more flexible destination LNG cargoes diverted from Europe to Asia to meet demand and arbitrage opportunities . Europe relying on storage top-up + pipeline gas (Russia?) + alternative suppliers (US LNG, Australia, Africa). Substitution is geographic (not volumetric) — total supply constrained, not redirectable.
Why this matters Substitution pathways exist at margins for aluminium and naphtha but are time-consuming (recycling plant qualification 6–9 months, new cracker ramp 3–6 months, Asia import logistics 4–8 weeks). Aluminium has best substitution pathway (recycled + Jebel Ali continuation + overseas inventory); naphtha has worst (teapot overcapacity but low quality, Chinese new capacity ongoing oversupply). LNG substitution is zero-sum (geographic reallocation, not new supply) — Europe left short.
Implication Aluminium supply chain is most resilient (Jebel Ali + inventory + recycling = 40–50% of prior Al Taweelah gap can be filled by Day 84). Naphtha supply chain is least resilient (Korean restructuring = permanent 10–15% capacity reduction, Chinese overcapacity = deflationary pressure, Middle East exporting nations = customers lose). LNG supply chain is medium resilience (Europe storage + pipeline emergency protocols will kick in Q3 2026; Asia secure; buyers accept higher prices). Wave 3 cascade tail compressed for aluminium, extended for naphtha/petrochem, medium for LNG.

Methodology

This brief uses a tiered signal-weighting framework. Only verified physical events from primary sources can move the Wave Intensity assessment. Trend movement requires confirmed change versus the prior 72 hours. The system continuously calibrates against outcomes — when predictions miss, the underlying heuristics are sharpened. The full operating ruleset is kept private to preserve analytical edge.

Sources

Inputs are graded across six tiers before they can move the assessment. Specific outlets are not published.

Tier 1 — Primary, official, verifiable: operator press releases and FM letters; stock-exchange filings; regulator notices; sovereign and central-bank communications; AIS-confirmed vessel movements.
Tier 2 — Specialised commercial intelligence with multi-year operational track record across energy, petrochemicals, maritime, and metals desks.
Tier 3 — Analytical institutions and multilateral commodity desks with rigorous publication standards.
Tier 4 — Commercial analysts and named columnists with auditable forecast records.
Tier 5 — Wire services and reputable regional / sector press.
Tier 6 — Excluded by default: anonymous OSINT, op-eds, and AI-summarised aggregator output. Admitted only with independent Tier 1–3 corroboration.
